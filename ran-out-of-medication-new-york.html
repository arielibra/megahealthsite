Ran Out of Medication Before Your Doctor Renewed It? Your Options in New York

Medically reviewed by Dr. Sam Borukhov, PharmD
Owner & Supervising Pharmacist · Reviewed September 29, 2026

If you have run out of a medication, call your pharmacy and say you are out, not just low. Your pharmacist can contact your doctor for an urgent renewal and, in limited situations, New York law allows a pharmacist to provide a short emergency supply of certain non-controlled medications when your doctor can’t be reached.

First: is it urgent?

If you have symptoms like chest pain, trouble breathing, a seizure, severe confusion or thoughts of harming yourself, call 911 (or 988 for a mental health crisis). For medications like insulin, seizure medications, blood thinners, heart medications and inhalers, running out can be dangerous — treat it as urgent.

Your options for an emergency prescription refill

  1. Call your pharmacy. Say you are out. We contact your doctor and mark it urgent.
  2. Ask about an emergency supply. In limited circumstances, New York pharmacists can dispense a short emergency supply of certain non-controlled maintenance medications when the prescriber can’t be reached. It depends on the medication and your history.
  3. Call your doctor’s after-hours line for a same-day electronic prescription.
  4. Telehealth or urgent care can often renew a routine maintenance medication.
  5. Traveling? Your prescription can often be transferred to a pharmacy near you.

Controlled medications have stricter rules and generally cannot be supplied without a valid prescription.

How to never run out again

  • Automatic refills — we fill before you run out.
  • Renewal tracking — when you are down to your last refill, we request a new prescription from your doctor before you need it.
  • 90-day supplies for maintenance medications.
  • Medication syncing so everything is due on one day.
  • Free delivery so a busy week doesn’t mean a missed pickup.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I ran out of my medication?

Call your pharmacy and say you are out. They can contact your doctor urgently and, in limited cases, may be able to provide a short emergency supply.

Can a pharmacist give an emergency refill in New York?

In limited circumstances, New York pharmacists can dispense a short emergency supply of certain non-controlled medications when the prescriber cannot be reached.

How do I avoid running out?

Use automatic refills, let your pharmacy track renewals, ask for 90-day supplies, and sync your medications.
