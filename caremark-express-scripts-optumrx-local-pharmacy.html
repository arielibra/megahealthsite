Can I Use CVS Caremark, Express Scripts or OptumRx at a Local Pharmacy?

Usually, yes. CVS Caremark, Express Scripts and OptumRx are pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), not pharmacies. Most of their plans include independent pharmacies like Mega Health Rx in their networks. Some employer plans add rules for certain drugs, so it is worth a one-minute check.

What a PBM is

A pharmacy benefit manager runs the prescription part of your health insurance. It decides which drugs are covered (the formulary), what you pay, and which pharmacies are in network. The three largest are CVS Caremark, Express Scripts (owned by Cigna) and OptumRx (owned by UnitedHealth Group).

Having a Caremark card does not mean you have to fill at a CVS store, just as an Express Scripts card doesn’t tie you to one pharmacy.

OptumRx vs. CVS Caremark: what is different for you?

OptumRx and CVS Caremark do the same job: they manage the prescription side of your insurance. OptumRx belongs to UnitedHealth Group, and CVS Caremark belongs to CVS Health.

For patients, the differences come from your employer’s plan, not from the PBM itself: which drugs are covered, your copay, and whether certain medications must go through mail order or a specialty pharmacy. Two people with OptumRx cards can have very different rules. The fastest way to know yours is a test claim, and we can run one in about a minute.

Rules that sometimes apply

  • Maintenance medication rules. Some employer plans allow only a few fills of a long-term medication at a retail pharmacy before requiring mail order or a 90-day supply at specific pharmacies.
  • Specialty drugs. High-cost specialty medications may have to go through the PBM’s specialty pharmacy.
  • Preferred networks. Some plans have lower copays at preferred pharmacies.

These rules vary by employer and plan. Your card does not tell you which apply — but we can check in about a minute.
